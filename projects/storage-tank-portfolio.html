03

What I Engineer

Capacity & Geometry

Translating the required working capacity and service into practical tank dimensions and an arrangement that fits the available package footprint.

Nozzles & Access

Defining fill, drain, vent, access and equipment connection locations so the tank can interface correctly with the complete mechanical system.

Supports & Integration

Developing support arrangements and checking the tank's relationship with skids, piping, pumps, instruments and customer connection points.

Manufacturing Drawings

Converting the design into clear 2D engineering information with dimensions, details and fabrication-oriented documentation.

04

Engineering Workflow

Requirement→Capacity→Geometry→Nozzle & Access Layout→Supports→3D Model→2D Drawings→MTO / BOM→Fabrication Support
05

Package-Level Thinking

My tank-design work is not limited to producing a vessel shape. The tank is treated as one component of the complete mechanical package.

This means considering how piping routes connect to the tank, how access and maintenance areas remain usable, how instruments and valves are positioned, and how the finished assembly can be fabricated and integrated.

Design approach3D-first mechanical integration
Primary toolsSolidWorks · AutoCAD
Project interfacePiping · Pumps · Instruments · Skids
Execution focusDesign → Drawings → Fabrication
